# Funnel

Criteria and findings on the left rule out options in the middle, and the survivors flow into the decision on the right. It's the elimination story of a decision.

## What was tried

- **Family trip → Why options fell out.** Layered left to right over `rules-out`, `option-for`, `chosen`, `corrects` and `refines`, limited to criteria, claims, things and the decision. "Cape rentals use fixed weekly changeovers" knocks out three Cape houses; "Every available Cape pool home is over $20k" knocks out five; "Region C owners don't heat pools" two.
- **3D printer → Decision graph.** The same data radially around "Which printer should we buy?", with criteria verdicts and the claims that knocked options out (`blocks`, `corrects`).
- **3D printer → Money.** Ways to make money, with compliance risks (FCC, CPSC, copyright) that `block` some of them. Funnel-shaped, but about ideas rather than options.

## Why it fell flat

- The Comparison Table already told the elimination story better: the red cells *are* the reasons, and the table keeps options aligned.
- The layered layout produced long crossing edges, because one finding rules out many options and one option fails many criteria.
- Findings and criteria mixed on the left, so "a requirement we set" and "a fact we learned" looked the same.

## Ideas for the next pass

- Order by *when* each option fell out (stage 1: changeover, stage 2: budget, …): a real funnel with stages, not a DAG.
- Group ruled-out options per reason, so each reason is one box with its casualties inside.
- Maybe this is a mode of the Comparison Table (sort by elimination stage) rather than its own View Type.
